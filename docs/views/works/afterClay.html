Project Notes

After Clay began when I worked with clay for the first time. I was struck by how cracks or breakage during firing are often treated as failure. Clay comes from geological processes that take an immense amount of time, and I began to wonder whether breaking and cracking could also be understood as part of the material itself rather than something that must always be avoided.

This made me reconsider the way I had been working with discarded newspaper. I had often tried to overcome its weakness, but its short fibres also make it possible to form extremely thin surfaces. I began experimenting with this fragility rather than trying to eliminate it.

At first, I formed paper pulp over positive plaster moulds so that it could dry quickly. Later, I began using unfired clay forms themselves as reusable moulds, avoiding the need to produce additional plaster moulds. By applying a mixture of discarded newspaper and bio-based material directly onto the clay, I was able to create lightweight, translucent shells as thin as approximately 0.1 mm.

After Clay explores how the limitations of one material can become the starting point for another way of making.
