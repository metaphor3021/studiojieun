Visual Silence is a pair of lights that gives the user control over the first level of light encountered after darkness.
Through tactile dimming and brightness memory, the lamps soften the transition into light while keeping the surrounding darkness present.

Project Notes

Visual Silence began with my sensitivity to light. At night, once my eyes have adjusted to darkness, switching on a light can make the whole space feel visually loud. What overwhelms me is not simply brightness itself, but the sudden transition from darkness to light especially when I have no control over the level I first encounter.

This led me to ask: at what brightness, and at what pace, do I want to first encounter light? Both lamps are controlled through touch rather than a visible switch. The user finds the touch point by hand and holds it to adjust the brightness. The lamps remember the last setting, allowing the next encounter with light to begin at a level chosen beforehand.

The forms were developed to keep the light contained rather than allowing it to spread freely through the room. The wall light provides a larger vertical reference for orientation, while the smaller table light keeps the glow close to the object and the hand. In both, the light source remains hidden and appears only through a narrow illuminated area.

Paper and lacquer, materials central to my practice, also shape the experience of the light. The dark, irregular surfaces absorb visual attention, while layers of lacquered hanji conceal and soften the LED source. The glow appears embedded within the material rather than projected outward.

Visual Silence was informed by the logic of noise cancelling not to eliminate light, but to reduce the intensity of what can feel like visual noise. It is not simply about making light dimmer, but about having control over how light is first encountered after darkness. That quieter relationship with light is what I mean by Visual Silence.
